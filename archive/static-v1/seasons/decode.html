DECODE

A rebuilt swerve chassis, a tested intake and an auto-aim launcher — AE86’s fourth season, documented from first failure to field-ready system.

01 / Overview 02 / Robot & software 03 / Competition Record 04 / Members

01 / Overview

We kept the risky idea — and rebuilt it properly.

The first swerve build failed on belt and assembly tolerances. We rebuilt all four modules, measured the intake changes and connected odometry to the launcher’s aiming system.

02 / Robot & software

What changed, and why it worked.

The CAD views show the complete machine. The three systems below explain the rebuilds and measurements that shaped the field-ready robot.

Chassis / rebuild

Swerve, iteration two

After belt and assembly tolerances ended the first build, the team rebuilt all four modules with replaceable FDM parts and one consistent assembly process. Analog angle feedback and PID steering are present in the current code.

Intake / measured

29 → 43 game pieces

A movable roller added downward and horizontal force, while elastic belts and a dual-servo deflector reduced jams. In the documented comparison, intake rose from 29 to 43 and scoring from 23 to 38.

Launcher / auto-aim

Range through control

Dual flywheels, harder silicone wheels and a 200 g counterweight stabilize the shot. A servo guide plate, encoder feedback, voltage compensation and odometry-based aiming adapt it to field position.
